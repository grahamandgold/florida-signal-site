You just gained a second set of eyes on every permit, deed and vote in Broward County.

Welcome, and thank you for subscribing. I’m glad you’re here.

I’ve spent 25 years as a journalist, and I’ve learned that the most consequential stories in a city rarely announce themselves. They surface quietly: a permit filing, a recorded deed, a line item on a Tuesday agenda. South Florida is one of the most important development markets in the country, and the people working in it deserve to see those signals first.

Too often, we learn about a deal or development weeks after the paperwork first showed something was moving. By then, the people closest to the market may already know. Florida Signal is built to narrow that gap. I’m working to bring you current, record-backed intelligence as the paperwork lands, before it becomes an old headline.

That’s what Florida Signal is for. Stories that have impact, and intelligence that helps you do your job, whether you develop, broker, sell or own.

WHAT YOU’LL GET
 
01
The Monday Signal
A weekly development-intelligence brief built from permits, deeds, public votes and other records across Fort Lauderdale and Broward County. As coverage expands, so will the frequency.
 
02
The data room, being built now
I’m building one connected research library behind this brief: more than 134,000 Fort Lauderdale permit records, 210,000 Broward recording documents and 2.5 million permit workflow events, plus property records, city and county agendas, corporate filings, FAA construction notices and environmental permits. The goal is simple: connect records that normally live in separate systems so changes surface sooner.
 
03
The receipt comes with the story
Every published Signal points back to the records behind it. Facts are separated from analysis, and when the record doesn’t answer a question, we’ll tell you that too.
Facts from named records · analysis and unknowns labeled · corrections public
